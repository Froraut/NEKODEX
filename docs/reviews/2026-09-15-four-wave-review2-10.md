## New potential defect: 1

**R2-10-1 — Failed bounded cleanup can leave the helper alive (P2, conditional).** Trigger: after a `verify`, `inspect`, or `smoke` result/error/timeout, the helper receives `shutdown` but `closeChatGptBrowserWorkers()` does not finish within five seconds; the parent sends `SIGTERM`, and worker closure still does not finish in the following two seconds. At [browser-helper-verifier.cjs](../../launcher/electron/browser-helper-verifier.cjs#L42), lines 42–51, `stopChild` throws after the second wait, without further process termination or retained cleanup ownership. The helper catches `SIGTERM` by invoking the *same* asynchronous shutdown at [browser-helper-main.ts](../../src/adapters/chatgpt-web/browser-helper-main.ts#L118), lines 118–154 and 534–539; `process.exit` occurs only after `closeChatGptBrowserWorkers()` settles. That function waits for every `worker.close()` at [browser-worker.ts](../../src/adapters/chatgpt-web/browser-worker.ts#L112), lines 112–121. Consequence: the caller gets a cleanup failure, but the child can continue running with open resources; a successful connector selection is reported as failed if cleanup alone is late. `browser-host.cjs:3108-3125` propagates that failure, and `main.cjs:670-702` clears `mcpSetupComplete`. Counterevidence: ordinary shutdown exits within the five-second grace; a slow close may eventually finish, and the worker close path may itself have bounds. No stalled worker or orphan was reproduced. This is a conditional lifecycle failure, not a wrong Native4/Zero Risk4 identity or a public-schema problem. A correction would need to retain exact child ownership until exit or enforce a final bounded termination while preserving the original verification error.

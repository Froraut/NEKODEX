## Correction

Wave 1 correctly retained exact helper child handles after the bounded graceful,
SIGTERM, and SIGKILL observations, and it serialized helper operations through a
single process-wide tail. The process-wide ownership was too broad for the
current launcher: `AccountBrowserPool` creates one `BrowserHost` and one
descriptor path per account. A slow or unobservable helper for account A could
therefore block an independent inspection or verification for account B, and
ordinary operations for separate browser scopes were forced into one queue.

The verifier now keeps a small internal scope registry keyed by the supplied
`descriptorPath`:

- operations sharing one descriptor remain serialized;
- retained child handles are reconciled only against operations in that same
  descriptor scope;
- an unsettled child in one account cannot reject a helper operation for another
  account;
- the scope entry is released after its queued operations finish and every
  retained exact child handle has emitted `exit` or `close`, including a late
  child exit after the original operation already failed.

The existing exact-child shutdown sequence, primary-error precedence,
`operationId`/`cleanupError` reporting, helper protocol checks, connector-name
checks, exports, and public `Native4`, `Native4 DEV`, and `ZeroRisk4` ABI remain
unchanged.

## Review and limits

Manual source review covered the final verifier diff, `BrowserHost` direct
callers, `AccountBrowserPool`'s per-account descriptor construction, the helper
shutdown contract, and the accepted D16 correction records. No optional
redesign was introduced. The change is source-level; it does not prove live
Chromium/Playwright teardown timing, OS signal behavior, or ownership survival
after the launcher process itself terminates. The descriptor path is treated as
the browser-host scope identity supplied by the existing callers.
